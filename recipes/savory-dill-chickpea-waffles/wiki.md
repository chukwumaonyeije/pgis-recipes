## Savory Dill Chickpea Waffles

These savory dill chickpea waffles convert a high-protein chickpea batter into a batch-friendly breakfast or recovery meal that aligns more closely with PGIS principles than conventional waffles. Instead of leaning on refined flour, sugar, and syrup, the recipe uses chickpea flour, soy milk, flaxseed, and optional tofu to create a savory, fiber-forward, protein-supportive structure that can function as a repeatable meal rather than a novelty breakfast.

The double batch yields about 16 waffles, making it particularly useful for meal prep. The recipe is designed to remain flexible: oil can be reduced for a stricter insulin-sensitivity protocol, tofu can be added for a higher-protein version, and the flavor profile can be pushed more savory with garlic powder, turmeric, or nutritional yeast.

### FULL INGREDIENTS LIST

#### Wet ingredients

- 4 cups plain unsweetened soy milk
- 4 tablespoons apple cider vinegar
- 6 tablespoons ground flaxseed
- 1 cup water
- 1/2 cup grapeseed or canola oil, or less for a lower-fat PGIS or Mastering Diabetes version

#### Dry ingredients

- 3 cups chickpea flour
- 1 cup oat flour, whole wheat pastry flour, or a blend
- 2 teaspoons baking powder
- 1 teaspoon baking soda
- 2 teaspoons salt
- 2 to 4 teaspoons sugar, optional
- 2 tablespoons dried dill
- 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper

#### Optional protein boost

- 1 cup silken tofu, blended smooth
- or 1 1/2 cups firm tofu, very finely crumbled or blended

#### Optional upgrades

- Turmeric
- Garlic powder
- Nutritional yeast

#### Suggested serving options

- Avocado spread if fat is not being restricted
- Lentil topping
- Savory unsweetened plant-based yogurt
- Greens or chopped herbs

### METHOD

1. Whisk the soy milk and apple cider vinegar together and let the mixture stand for 10 minutes to create a buttermilk-style base.
2. In a separate bowl, mix the ground flaxseed with the water and let it sit for 5 minutes until gelled.
3. If using tofu, blend it until smooth. Add a few tablespoons of soy milk if needed to help it blend.
4. Combine the wet ingredients: flax mixture, curdled soy milk, oil, and optional tofu. Whisk until smooth.
5. In a large bowl, whisk together the chickpea flour, oat flour, baking powder, baking soda, salt, optional sugar, dried dill, and black pepper.
6. Add the wet mixture to the dry ingredients and stir gently until just combined.
7. Rest the batter for 5 to 10 minutes. The target texture is thick and slow-pouring.
8. Adjust the batter if necessary with 1 tablespoon of soy milk if it is too thick or 1 tablespoon of flour if it is too loose.
9. Preheat the waffle iron thoroughly and lightly oil the plates if needed.
10. Cook the waffles until deep golden and crisp, usually slightly longer than standard waffles.
11. Cool briefly on a rack before stacking or storing.

### PGIS NOTES

**Chickpea flour** is the core metabolic advantage of the recipe. It offers a legume-based carbohydrate source with more fiber and protein than standard refined-flour waffles, helping the meal behave more like a structured savory breakfast than a dessert.

**Soy milk and optional tofu** raise total protein and improve satiety. They also help the waffles fit better into a recovery meal or more sustained clinic-day breakfast pattern.

**Flaxseed** contributes binding, fiber, and texture support. Combined with chickpea flour, it slows the meal down relative to low-fiber breakfast waffles.

**Dill, pepper, and savory add-ins** change the practical use case. A savory waffle is often easier to pair with lentils, greens, tofu, or plant yogurt, which helps keep the meal inside a lower-swing PGIS pattern.

**Oil** is the main adjustable tradeoff. The listed amount improves browning and texture, but the recipe can be pushed toward a stricter lower-fat metabolic strategy by cutting the oil down and ensuring the waffle iron is well preheated.

### HOW TO USE IT

**Onyeije Breakfast System savory variant:** Use 2 waffles with savory plant yogurt, chopped herbs, and a lentil or bean side.

**Post-workout recovery meal:** Pair 2 to 3 waffles with tofu, lentils, or soy yogurt after training when insulin sensitivity is better and a savory option is more appealing than sweet foods.

**Clinic-day breakfast:** Keep a batch in the refrigerator or freezer and reheat in a toaster for a fast, repeatable breakfast that feels substantial without depending on processed convenience food.

### CGM STRATEGY NOTE

This recipe should generally outperform conventional waffles because its carbohydrate load is embedded in a legume-and-whole-grain matrix with meaningful fiber and protein support. The exact response will still depend on the oil level, portion size, and toppings.

For a flatter curve, keep the serving savory. Lentils, greens, tofu, or unsweetened plant yogurt will usually preserve the intended PGIS structure better than sweet spreads or syrup. If glucose rises more than expected, first reduce the oil and tighten the topping strategy before changing the chickpea base itself.

### STORAGE AND REHEAT

- Refrigerate for up to 4 days.
- Freeze for up to 2 months.
- Reheat in a toaster or oven for the best texture recovery.

### PRACTICAL ADVANTAGE

The strongest value of this recipe is not novelty. It is repeatability. It turns a savory, protein-forward, plant-based breakfast into a format that can be prepared in bulk, stored easily, and reused across clinic days, recovery windows, and structured breakfast routines.
