## Double Tofu Stuffed Flatbreads

This recipe adds a more indulgent savory main to the PGIS plant-based bench: silken-tofu dough wrapped around a super-firm tofu filling seasoned with garlic, ginger, doubanjiang, soy sauce, rice vinegar, spring onions, and vegan cheese. It is more comfort-food-shaped than the lighter PGIS bowls and scrambles, but the dual-tofu structure still makes it more protein-forward than a standard stuffed bread.

### FULL INGREDIENTS LIST

#### Dough

- 350 g plain flour
- 300 g silken tofu
- pinch of salt

#### Filling

- 400 g super-firm tofu
- 4 cloves garlic
- 1 inch ginger
- 3 spring onions
- 1.5 tbsp brown sugar
- 2 tbsp doubanjiang
- 3 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1.5 tbsp rice vinegar
- sesame oil
- vegan cheese

#### Garnish

- spring onions
- sesame seeds
- chili oil

### METHOD

1. Mix the flour, silken tofu, and salt until the dough comes together. Knead until smooth and only lightly tacky, adding a little more flour if needed. Cover with a damp towel and rest for 30 minutes.
2. Heat a splash of sesame oil in a large frying pan or wok over medium-high heat. Fry the garlic, ginger, and white parts of the spring onions for about 1 minute.
3. Crumble in the super-firm tofu and cook for a few minutes to drive off excess moisture.
4. Whisk the brown sugar, doubanjiang, soy sauce, and rice vinegar together. Pour over the tofu mixture, stir to coat evenly, then remove from the heat and let it cool.
5. Divide the dough into 6 pieces and roll each into a ball. On a floured surface, roll each one out to a few millimeters thick.
6. Place one-sixth of the tofu filling in the center of each round, then add a generous handful of grated vegan cheese.
7. Bring the dough edges together over the filling, pinch closed, flip seam-side down, and gently roll or press each one flatter.
8. Cook in a dry non-stick pan over medium heat for about 3 minutes per side with the lid on until golden brown.
9. Brush with chili oil and finish with the green parts of the spring onions and sesame seeds.

### PGIS NOTES

**Dual-tofu structure** is the main metabolic advantage. Both the dough and the filling contribute protein support, so the finished dish behaves more like a savory composed meal than plain bread with flavoring.

**Flour, vegan cheese, and finishing oil** are the main energy-density tradeoffs. This is not one of the lighter PGIS defaults, so it works best as a planned meal rather than a casual snack spiral.

**Doubanjiang, soy sauce, and cheese** build excellent flavor but can raise sodium quickly. That matters most when portion size drifts upward or the meal is paired with other salty sides.

**Handheld format** improves adherence. This matters because a recipe that feels more satisfying and social can still serve PGIS goals if the portion strategy stays disciplined.

### HOW TO USE IT

**Post-training savory meal:** Use 1 flatbread with greens, curtido, or a lighter vegetable side after longer runs or strength work when a more substantial savory plate improves follow-through.

**Weekend batch prep:** Make the full batch and refrigerate the cooked flatbreads for reheating in a dry pan. The recipe is especially useful when the week needs one higher-satiety savory anchor meal.

**Shared meal context:** This is a good bridge recipe for serving others while staying plant-based and protein-aware, especially when a strict bowl-based meal would feel harder to sustain socially.

### CGM STRATEGY NOTE

Expected response is likely higher than lighter PGIS bowls, scrambles, or low-fat waffles because the flour and cheese increase density. The tofu base still makes the meal stronger than a standard stuffed bread, but portion size matters.

For a flatter curve, keep the serving to 1 flatbread, pair it with a large vegetable side, and use chili oil as a finishing accent rather than a heavy brush. If a sharper rise appears, first tighten cheese load, finishing oil, and accompaniment choices before abandoning the recipe entirely.

### PREP NOTES

- Let the filling cool before shaping so the dough seals more easily.
- Use enough bench flour to roll thinly without tearing, but avoid over-drying the dough.
- A test flatbread is worth cooking first because the shaping step came from a social-post video rather than a formal printed method.
- Reheat in a dry skillet rather than a microwave if texture matters.
