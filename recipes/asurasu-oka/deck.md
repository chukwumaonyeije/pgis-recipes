# Asurasu Oka

### Vegan · Oil-Free

Savory steamed corn pudding with fresh corn, onion, Scotch bonnet, and mushroom powder.

**Makes 4 individual puddings** · Four 8-ounce ramekins  
Prep: about 15 minutes · Steam: 60–90 minutes · Rest: 10 minutes

---

## The Adaptation

Asurasu Oka is a savory Igbo steamed corn pudding.

- Keep the **fresh corn, onion, and Scotch bonnet**.
- Replace ground crayfish with **finely ground dried shiitake** for savory depth.
- Omit the palm oil; steaming gives the pudding its tender texture.

Based on the supplied adaptation of a Plated Memories recipe.

---

## Ingredients · 4 Puddings

- **3 cups fresh yellow corn kernels**, from about 4–6 ears
- **1 small onion**, roughly chopped
- **¼ Scotch bonnet pepper** for gentle heat, or more to taste
- **1–2 teaspoons finely ground dried shiitake mushrooms**
- **¼ teaspoon salt**, optional
- **Up to ¼ cup finely ground whole-grain cornmeal**, as needed
- **Warm water**, a tablespoon at a time, only if needed for blending

Measure the kernels: ear size varies. Start with less pepper if heat-sensitive.

---

## Equipment and Setup

- Food processor or blender
- Four **ungreased, 8-ounce heatproof ramekins** or small steel pudding pots
- Foil or individual lids
- Large pot with a lid and a steaming rack

Set up the rack **above the water level**. The covered cups should sit in steam, with enough room for the pot lid to close.

---

## 1 · Blend the Corn

1. Cut the kernels into a bowl. Scrape the cobs with the back of a knife to collect the **milky pulp**.
2. Pulse kernels, pulp, onion, and Scotch bonnet until **mostly smooth**, with a little corn texture remaining.
3. Add warm water **one tablespoon at a time**, only if the blades struggle.
4. Stir in the mushroom powder and optional salt.

Use 1 teaspoon mushroom powder for a milder flavor, or up to 2 teaspoons for more savory depth.

---

## 2 · Adjust the Batter

**Target:** a spoonful forms a soft mound that slowly settles.

1. If the batter is loose, stir in whole-grain cornmeal **one tablespoon at a time**.
2. Let the cornmeal hydrate for **5 minutes** before deciding whether to add more.
3. Use only what is needed, up to **¼ cup total** for the batch.

Fresh sweet corn varies in moisture. Avoid adding extra blending water that will need thickening later.

---

## 3 · Steam for 60–90 Minutes

1. Divide batter evenly among the **four ungreased cups**. Leave room below each rim.
2. Cover each cup with foil or a lid and place on the rack **above simmering water**.
3. Cover the pot and maintain a steady simmer for **60–90 minutes**.
4. Check the water level occasionally. Replenish with **hot water** as needed, keeping the water below the cups.

Keep the pot from boiling dry. Open the lid away from your face to release steam.

---

## 4 · Check the Center and Rest

- Start checking at **60 minutes**.
- The center should feel **set**, and a skewer should come out **without wet batter**.
- If the center is wet, cover and continue steaming, then check again.
- Rest for **10 minutes** before serving warm by the spoonful.

Expect a tender steamed pudding. Serve directly from the cups.

---

## Build a Meal Bowl

Pair **one corn pudding** with:

- **Black-eyed peas**
- **Steamed ugu or spinach**
- **Oil-free tomato–pepper sauce**, spooned over the greens

The corn provides sweetness; Scotch bonnet brings heat. Peas and greens add variety, fiber, and protein to the meal.

---

## Approximate Carbohydrate

**One pudding = ¼ of the batch, before sides.**

| Batter version | Total carbohydrate per pudding |
|---|---:|
| Fresh corn thickens on its own | About 22 g |
| Full ¼ cup cornmeal added to the batch | About 29 g |

This is the **22–29 g estimate supplied with the recipe**, not a lab analysis. Corn variety, ingredient weights, and portion size affect the total. Count peas and sauce separately.

---

The carbohydrate estimate describes the pudding; individual glucose responses vary.
